Live sensor, last 10 minutes

Ground motion at the Barro Colorado station in the Panama Canal, as it reaches this computer. The thin line under it is the short/long average ratio. A trigger is not a clock tick: it happens when that ratio crosses the grey level (5), so triggers are as far apart as the earthquakes are (never less than 75 s). The shaded minute after each trigger is what gets measured: its largest motion is read as a magnitude, and an alert is written the moment that reading reaches the announcing level.

Recent earthquakes: who knew first

Delays count from the time the IGC gives the earthquake, which in its first minutes can be up to a minute late: "before" means the sensor had it before that time. Head start compares the two moments on this computer's clock. The sensor knows only that the ground moved and roughly how much; place and final size come from the agencies.
